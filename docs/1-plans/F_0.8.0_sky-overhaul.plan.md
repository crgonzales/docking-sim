# Sky & Cloud System Overhaul Implementation Plan

## Overview

Rebuild the planet's visual system — sun, atmosphere, clouds, and their shared
lighting — as one coherent, physically-derived architecture, replacing the
accreted per-layer approximations that produced the current "walmart version."
Target look: modded KSP (EVE volumetric clouds, Scatterer atmosphere). This plan
**supersedes the unfinished visual scope of `F_0.8.0_visual-pass.plan.md`**;
that document remains the record of what already landed (KTX2 pipeline, texture
tiers, models, livery, debug camera, credits). Same branch (`feat/visual-pass`),
same release (v0.8.0). Everything stays in `apps/web` — zero sim-core or
scenario changes.

## Problem Statement

Six user-verified defects, all traced to two root causes:

1. **Sun parallax** — the sun sprite sits at a fixed world point 40,000 units
   out (*inside* the scene); a 2,000-unit camera orbit swings it ~3° against
   the starfield. A sun is at optical infinity and must never move against the
   stars.
2. **Shadows miss their clouds by 2–5 cloud-widths** — `SHADOW_EXAGGERATION
   6.0` × 7 km deck displaces shadows 42–120 km while cloud features are
   8–22 km wide. The constant was calibrated against an earlier (wrong) 25.5 km
   altitude and never re-derived.
3. **Clouds read as bokeh, not cumulus** — puffs are bare radial-falloff discs;
   ~⅔ of the 12,000 instances land in clear sky and get GPU-gated to nothing,
   thinning the cloudy regions they were budgeted for.
4. **Deck drift is ~100× real** — 0.00035 rad/s is a 2.2 km/s ground speed,
   fast enough that world-anchored puffs visibly de-correlate from the deck
   within a minute.
5. **Per-layer terminators disagree** — surface (−0.12/0.18), deck
   (−0.16/0.20), and puffs (−0.14/0.22) each smoothstep their own day/night
   boundary; the terminator falls in a different place on each layer.
6. **The atmosphere is a formula, not a solution** — analytic single scatter
   plus ad-hoc ambient and ozone terms, versus Scatterer's precomputed
   radiative transfer. Missing entirely: aerial perspective on the surface,
   which is most of what makes a planet read as having air.

**Root causes.** (a) Physically coupled constants — altitude, shadow offset,
drift, puff size, band thickness, cap angle — live scattered across four files
as independent magic numbers; three separate incidents (wrong altitudes,
divergent coverage curves, stale shadow scale) came from exactly this. (b) No
shared lighting model; every material reinvents the sun.

## Solution Architecture

**One config, everything derived.** A single `skyConfig.ts` owns every physical
quantity in physical units (altitudes in km, drift as ground speed in m/s,
puff sizes in km) and *derives* every renderer-facing value from them in code
— radius multipliers, rad/s rates, shadow angular offsets, the volumetric cap
half-angle (computed from the camera envelope, not guessed). Changing an
altitude can no longer silently strand its shadow offset.

**One lighting include.** A shared GLSL module (pattern proven by
`cloudCoverage.ts`) defines the single terminator function, sun tint ramp, and
ambient floor. Surface, deck, cirrus, and volumetrics consume it — the
atmosphere shell is exempt, since its illumination comes from radiative
transfer (§2). Agreement across the surface-material layers becomes
structural, exactly as it did for cloud coverage.

**Atmosphere: precomputed, Hillaire-style.** A build-time Node script bakes a
transmittance LUT and a multiple-scattering LUT (Hillaire 2020, "A Scalable and
Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique") from real Earth
coefficients. The atmosphere shell raymarches a small fixed step count against
those LUTs; the Earth surface samples the same math for **aerial perspective**
(haze and blue-shift toward the limb). Baked artifacts are committed, so
deployment needs no extra build step and results are reproducible from the
script.

**Clouds: placed where clouds are.** Instance positions are rejection-sampled
on the CPU against a small coverage mask at startup — every one of the 12,000
instances lands in actual cloud, roughly tripling effective density at
identical cost. Puffs get procedural FBM shaping (no new texture), a
Henyey-Greenstein silver-lining term for back-lit edges near the terminator,
and two size octaves. Shadows, drift, altitudes, and the cap all come from the
config derivations.

**Sun at optical infinity.** Camera-relative anchoring (re-positioned to
`camera + SUN_DIR·D` each frame) gives exactly zero parallax; depth testing
still lets Earth occlude it, so orbiting behind the planet produces a true
sunrise. Correct 0.53° angular diameter; HDR disc for bloom glare; subtle
horizontal flare streak as a stretch goal.

## Implementation Details

### 1. `apps/web/src/scene/sky/skyConfig.ts` (new)

The single source of truth. Inputs (physical): deck altitude 7 km, cirrus
12.7 km, volumetric band 1.5–9 km, cloud ground speed 25 m/s (real jetstream
scale), puff sizes 8–22 km, instance count 12,000, shadow legibility factor 1.5
(documented as the one deliberate departure from geometry).

Atmosphere coefficients — the complete set, so the bake has a unique
implementation: **bottom radius 6371 km** (the scene's Earth), **top of
atmosphere 6471 km** (100 km shell); Rayleigh scattering
β = (5.802, 13.558, 33.1)×10⁻⁶ m⁻¹ (no absorption); **Mie split explicitly**:
scattering β_ms = 3.996×10⁻⁶ m⁻¹, absorption β_ma = 0.444×10⁻⁶ m⁻¹, extinction
β_mt = β_ms + β_ma, anisotropy g = 0.8; ozone: pure absorption
(0.650, 1.881, 0.085)×10⁻⁶ m⁻¹ over a tent profile centred 25 km, half-width
15 km, zero scattering; scale heights 8 km (Rayleigh) / 1.2 km (Mie).

Camera envelope constants live here too, because three derivations hang off
them: the **flight envelope** (CINEMATIC max orbit 2,000 units) drives the
volumetric cap — DEBUG is a diagnostic view and may see the cap boundary,
documented as intended; the **debug envelope** (DEBUG max orbit 40,000 units)
drives the sun anchor distance (§9).

Derived in code, with the derivation visible: radius multipliers from
altitudes; drift rad/s from ground speed ÷ Earth radius; shadow angular offset
from altitude × legibility ÷ radius; volumetric cap cosine from the camera
envelope (max orbit distance and Earth geometry — currently ≈61°, computed
not hardcoded). Existing constants in `Earth.tsx`, `Clouds.tsx`,
`VolumetricClouds.tsx`, and `cloudCoverage.ts` migrate here; the files import
what they need. `cloudCoverage.ts`'s GLSL and deck parameters fold in.

### 2. `apps/web/src/scene/sky/lighting.ts` (new)

Shared GLSL exported as a string (same pattern as `CLOUD_COVERAGE_GLSL`):
single terminator ramp, warm sun-tint near the terminator, one ambient/night
floor. Consumed by the **surface-material layers only**: Earth albedo blend,
both flat cloud layers, and the volumetric puff fragment. Every per-layer
smoothstep on `ndotl` in those materials is deleted in favour of these calls,
and the night floors (deck 0.04, puffs 0.08 — the grey-speck bug) become one
shared constant.

**The atmosphere shell is deliberately exempt.** Its illumination emerges from
radiative transfer — LUT transmittance and planet occlusion — and clamping it
to an artistic smoothstep would fight the physics it exists to provide. It
shares only `SUN_DIR` and the sun-tint convention. The same exemption applies
to the aerial-perspective term on the surface: physical, not ramped.

### 3. Atmosphere bake — `apps/web/scripts/bakeAtmosphere.mjs` (new) + LUT assets

Pure-math Node script (no GPU): bakes the transmittance LUT (256×64, RGB
float32) and the multiple-scattering LUT (32×32, RGB float32, Hillaire's
Ψ_ms). **Payload semantics, fixed here so bake, shader, and tests cannot
disagree: each transmittance texel stores T = exp(−τ) directly — a value in
(0, 1] per channel — never raw optical depth τ.** Storing T is standard
(Bruneton/Hillaire) because linear filtering of T behaves correctly while
filtered τ does not. The shader multiplies sampled T; it never exponentiates. Output as raw
`.bin` float32 committed to `apps/web/public/assets/lut/` (~200 KB total),
loaded at runtime into `DataTexture`s (FloatType, linear filter;
half-float fallback if `OES_texture_float_linear` is absent). The script is
committed and re-runnable; artifacts are committed so Pages deploys need no
bake step. The core math lives in an importable pure module so Vitest can test
it directly (see Test Impact).

### 4. Atmosphere shell rewrite — `Earth.tsx` atmosphere material

Replace the analytic fragment with a fixed 12-step raymarch through the shell:
per step, Rayleigh + Mie in-scatter weighted by the transmittance LUT toward
the sun and toward the camera, plus the multiple-scattering LUT contribution;
ozone absorption from the density tent. Phase functions: Rayleigh
`(1+cos²θ)`, Mie Henyey-Greenstein g=0.8 using the light-travel direction
(−sunDir — the sign convention that was previously verified). HDR contract
preserved: the terminator band may exceed luminance 1.0; the broad limb must
not.

### 4b. Sun-light extinction tint — the Scatterer signature

Once the transmittance LUT exists, sample it along the sun ray at the scene's
operating altitude each frame (one CPU-side lookup against the same baked
table, or a tiny uniform computed from it) and use the result to tint **the
directional light's color and the sun sprite** in lockstep. This is how
Scatterer makes craft and terrain bathe in reddened light at the terminator
while sky and lighting agree — one table drives both. Cheap (a per-frame
scalar lookup, no shader cost) and a large share of the modded-KSP sunset
look. The `SUN_DIR` authority and the lighting include's tint convention are
the integration points; the raw light color moves from a hardcoded `#fff6e8`
to the LUT-derived value.

### 5. Aerial perspective — `Earth.tsx` surface shader

The surface fragment computes camera→ground transmittance and in-scatter from
the same LUTs (4-step march or the LUT-difference shortcut): distant ground
hazes toward blue-white at the limb, near-nadir ground stays clear. This term
is what visually separates "textured ball" from "planet with air" and is
currently absent entirely.

### 6. Cloud placement — coverage mask + rejection sampling

A 512×256 grayscale coverage mask PNG is loaded at startup, read once via
canvas `ImageData`, and used to rejection-sample the 12,000 instance
positions: sample the cap, accept with probability proportional to coverage
(seeded RNG — deterministic across loads). Every instance lands in real
cloud. GPU density gating remains as the fine-grained term; placement stops
wasting the budget on empty sky.

**Generator toolchain (the repo has no Node image codec, and the only cloud
asset is KTX2, which Node cannot decode):** `makeCloudMask.mjs` uses two
pure-JS devDependencies — `utif2` (TIFF decode) and `pngjs` (PNG encode), no
native builds — added to `apps/web/package.json` (+ lockfile). Source input is
the NASA product the 4k map came from:
`https://eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov/images/imagerecords/57000/57747/cloud_combined_8192.tif`
(public domain); the script downloads it to a gitignored `scripts/.cache/` if
absent and verifies a SHA-256 recorded in the script. The committed mask is
the artifact; CI and deploys never run the generator.

**The mask stores FINAL coverage, not raw texels:** each mask pixel is the
result of the same transfer function the GPU applies, implemented once in
TypeScript as `cloudCoverageAtCpu()` in the sky module, sharing its constants
with the GLSL through `skyConfig`, and pinned by a test so CPU placement and
GPU gating cannot drift apart.

### 7. Puff quality — `VolumetricClouds.tsx` fragment/vertex

- **FBM shaping**: 2–3 octave procedural value noise (seeded per-instance from
  `instanceSeed`, no texture) modulates the radial falloff so each puff has a
  lumpy cauliflower silhouette instead of a disc.
- **Silver lining**: per-puff Henyey-Greenstein forward-scatter term (g≈0.65)
  on the light-travel direction brightens back-lit puff edges near the
  terminator — the single most recognisable EVE signature.
- **Two size octaves**: seed-based split into large cores (14–22 km) and small
  detail puffs (6–10 km) for silhouette variety.
- Lighting through the shared include; night floor from the shared constant.

### 7b. Surface relief & living water (owner-directed addition)

Two changes that buy more perceived detail than raising texture resolution:

- **Land relief via a baked normal map.** A committed script derives a
  4096×2048 tangent-space normal map from NASA's SRTM-derived global
  topography (`srtm_ramp2.world` product, public domain — recorded with URL +
  checksum in `ASSETS.md`) using a Sobel gradient, encoded UASTC KTX2 with
  offline mips and **linear color space** (normals are data — the spec-mask
  rule applies). The surface shader perturbs the lighting normal for the
  **day-lighting and glint terms only**, masked to land (`1 − waterMask`);
  the shared terminator ramp keeps using the geometric normal so the day/night
  line cannot disagree between relief-shaded land and everything else.
  Mountains catch grazing light at the terminator — the single biggest
  "textured ball → terrain" step. **Scope boundary, stated so it is never
  over-promised:** a normal map is lighting only — it does not displace
  geometry, so it changes no silhouette and produces no parallax. Correct and
  convincing from the LEO envelope; it will NOT survive a future
  atmosphere-to-surface descent, which requires a chunked-LOD displaced
  terrain (KSP's PQS approach) plus sub-resolution detail noise — the GEBCO
  elevation source baked here (≈1.85 km/sample) is deliberately the
  foundation dataset for that future system.
- **KSP-style living water.** No new texture: a procedural two-band scrolling
  normal perturbation (two directions, different spatial frequencies and
  speeds) applied where `waterMask` is high, feeding fresnel rim brightening
  and a sharpened, now time-varying sun glint. Time is a render-side
  wall-clock uniform — the same rule as cloud drift; nothing touches sim time.

### 8. Shadows, drift, cirrus — config-driven corrections

- Shadow offset derived from deck altitude × legibility 1.5 (~10 km at high
  sun — hugging its cloud; naturally stretching toward the terminator via the
  existing 1/ndotl term).
- Drift from 25 m/s ground speed (imperceptible in-session, honest in
  time-lapse); cirrus counter-drift similarly derived.
- Cirrus stops being "the same map rescaled": it samples a thin-coverage band
  of the map (smoothstep window over low densities, which is where real cirrus
  lives in the combined NASA product) at its own scale — documented as an
  approximation pending a dedicated cirrus source.

### 9. Sun — `SunSprite.tsx`

Camera-relative anchor each frame (zero parallax = optical infinity); correct
0.53° angular diameter derived from anchor distance; HDR disc (deliberate
bloom highlight per the contract) inside a sub-bloom halo; depth test on so
Earth and craft occlude it.

**Anchor distance is derived, not chosen** — a fixed 40,000 breaks occlusion:
in DEBUG the camera itself ranges to 40,000 units, putting Earth's far limb up
to ~53,000 units from the camera — *behind* a 40,000-unit sun, which would
then render in front of the planet. The config derives
`sunAnchorDistance = debugMaxOrbit + earthCenterDistance + earthRadius +
margin` (≈60,000 with current values; the 5×10⁷ far plane accommodates it), so
occlusion holds everywhere any camera can reach. Stretch: a faint horizontal
flare streak, additive, far below bloom threshold.

### 10. Migration & deletion

`Clouds.tsx`, `VolumetricClouds.tsx`, and the Earth cloud-shadow block lose
their local constants to `skyConfig.ts`; `cloudCoverage.ts` folds into the sky
module (exports preserved or re-exported); dead `cloudRadius` uniform removed.
The unfinished items of `F_0.8.0_visual-pass.plan.md` (glTF flag gate + livery
reprojection, hardware verification) migrate to this plan's Phase D unchanged.

## Technical Considerations

- **Pattern Usage**: shared-GLSL-include pattern proven by `cloudCoverage.ts`;
  module-level shader constants + `useMemo` materials + allocation-free
  `useFrame` per the established scene style; single-source config mirrors the
  bindings-table pattern (one table, all consumers).
- **Package Boundary**: entirely `apps/web` + committed assets/scripts. No
  sim-core or scenario imports or changes. Cloud drift and sun anchoring are
  render-side; nothing reads sim time or writes any store.
- **Conventions**: Hill frame as-is; Earth at −x̂ via the scaled group —
  untouched. Physical inputs in SI-adjacent units (km, m/s) at the config
  boundary, derived to renderer units in code. `SUN_DIR` remains the single
  lighting authority.
- **Determinism**: rejection sampling and FBM seeds use the existing seeded-RNG
  style — identical placement across loads. Bake script is deterministic;
  committed artifacts must match a re-run (verified by test).
- **Verification**: bake math gets real oracle tests (see Test Impact) — this
  is math, not vibes. Visual result is verified by the Phase D checkpoint
  protocol on the owner's hardware; SwiftShader screenshots are explicitly not
  acceptance evidence (they have misled twice this release).
- **Performance**: owner has explicitly deprioritized frame cost in favour of
  visual quality (30 fps informal target, not a gate). The FPS counter ships;
  the plan names the knobs (instance count, raymarch steps, aerial-perspective
  steps) but does not gate on them.
- **Edge Cases**: LUT float-texture support fallback (half-float); UV seam and
  poles in mask sampling; camera inside the atmosphere shell at close range;
  sun occlusion at the exact limb (bloom bleed); mask↔4k-map registration
  (same source, same projection — verified by a test comparing downsampled 4k
  against the mask); HMR — no more per-constant saves into a live session; the
  batched-save discipline stands.

## Performance & Cost Impact

Adds: 12-step shell raymarch over a small screen region, ~4-step aerial
perspective in the surface shader, FBM (≈6 noise evaluations) per puff
fragment, two 200 KB LUT textures. Removes: wasted ⅔ of volumetric instances.
Net: heavier fragments, better-spent instances. Frame cost is explicitly not a
gate per the owner; the FPS counter reports it and the knobs are named.

## Test Impact

- **New oracle tests (pure math, house style)**: transmittance monotonically
  decreasing with path length; β channel ordering blue > green > red preserved
  through the bake; energy bound (scattered ≤ incident); golden-value spot
  checks against published Earth transmittance figures; bake determinism
  (re-run equals committed artifact hash).
- **Config derivation tests**: multiplier↔altitude round-trip; drift from
  ground speed; shadow offset from altitude; cap cosine from camera envelope;
  sun anchor distance exceeds `debugMaxOrbit + earthCenterDistance +
  earthRadius` by the configured margin (the occlusion-bug oracle).
- **Placement tests**: rejection sampler determinism under fixed seed; all
  accepted samples above coverage threshold; acceptance density proportional
  to coverage within tolerance; mask↔4k-map registration check;
  `cloudCoverageAtCpu()` pinned against the GLSL constants so the CPU and GPU
  transfer functions cannot drift.
- **Existing tests**: all 130 must pass untouched — any sim-core or scenario
  movement is a defect signal, as in the parent plan.
- **Not unit-testable**: shader output — covered by the Phase D protocol and
  recorded in `COVERAGE-DEBT.md` per policy.
