# Volumetric Clouds Implementation Plan

## Overview

Replace the EVE-style cloud stack (two textured sphere shells plus 12,000
instanced billboards) with a physically-based raymarched volumetric cloud
system in the Nubis/Horizon lineage: Perlin–Worley 3D noise shaped by a
multi-channel weather map, Henyey-Greenstein scattering, and an
energy-conserving multi-scatter approximation, marched at a tier-selected
resolution with optional temporal reprojection.

The cloud march is the third of three phases. The first two are prerequisites,
not scope creep: cloud samples must be lit from the same transmittance and
multiple-scattering LUTs that `atmosphereMath.ts` already bakes, which is only
meaningful once every layer shares one radiance space and one exposure.

## Problem Statement

Three defects in the current renderer make correct cloud lighting impossible,
and they are the direct cause of two live bugs (a screen flash on atmospheric
entry, and terrain that is shaded inconsistently with the sky above it).

1. **No single radiance space.** Every layer applies its own display exposure
   multiplier from `SKY_CONFIG.exposure` (`groundIntensity: 100`,
   `spaceIntensity: 26`, `insideIntensity: 3`), selected per-shader by camera
   altitude. Terrain is therefore lit on a scale roughly 33× different from the
   sky it sits under, and any discontinuity in the selection reads as a flash.
2. **No tonemap stage.** `Effects.tsx` runs an `EffectComposer` with Bloom and
   documents an HDR contract (bloom threshold at luminance 1.0, "day albedo
   stays below by construction"), but nothing maps scene radiance to display.
   Each shader improvises, including ad-hoc `1 - exp(-x)` rolloffs in some
   paths and none in others.
3. **Surface lighting is hand-drawn, not sampled.** `lighting.ts` supplies
   `skyLightingAmount()` = `mix(0.04, 1.0, smoothstep(-0.12, 0.18, ndotl))` — a
   painted terminator with a constant `0.04` standing in for the entire sky —
   plus a hardcoded `skySunTint()` sunset lerp and a hardcoded
   `AERIAL_SKY_RADIANCE = [0.10, 0.18, 0.33]`. The atmosphere LUTs that could
   supply all of this correctly are baked, committed, and used only by the sky.

Consequently a large share of the existing cloud machinery (the deck rim-fade
hack, `throughLayerFog`, the cirrus band-selection, the removed
`instanceCoverage` floor) exists to paper over the lighting mismatch rather
than to model clouds. Replacing the representation without fixing the
foundation would reproduce that scaffolding in a more expensive form.

## Solution Architecture

**Phase 1 — one radiance space, one exposure, one tonemap.**
All shaders output scene-referred radiance normalized so that top-of-atmosphere
solar irradiance is 1.0. A single frame-global exposure scalar is computed once
per frame on the CPU as a continuous function of sun elevation and camera
altitude, and a tonemap pass is added to the existing composer. Every
per-layer exposure constant is deleted. Because the exposure is one value
applied uniformly and is continuous by construction, the flash class of bug
cannot recur.

Deliberately choosing an analytic per-frame exposure over measured
(histogram/readback) auto-exposure: it needs no GPU readback, stays
deterministic, and is a single continuous function. Measured auto-exposure with
eye adaptation is recorded as a follow-up, not built here.

**Phase 2 — surface and cloud lighting sampled from the atmosphere LUTs.**
`lighting.ts` gains `skySunIrradiance()` (transmittance LUT) and
`skyAmbientIrradiance()` (multiple-scattering LUT). Terrain shading becomes
`albedo/π × (skySunIrradiance × NdotL + skyAmbientIrradiance)` — the canonical
formula of §5, with exactly one transmittance factor, carried inside
`skySunIrradiance`. The painted
terminator, the sunset tint, and the hardcoded aerial colour are deleted;
soft terminators, reddened low sun, and blue-lit shadows then fall out of the
LUTs instead of being drawn by hand. The *existing* shell/billboard clouds are
moved onto the same lighting in this phase, so Phase 2 is independently
shippable and visually verifiable before any representation changes.

**Phase 3 — the volumetric march.**
Two marched bands (cumulus ~1.5–9 km and cirrus ~12.7 km — owner selected
fully volumetric for both). Density comes from a baked Perlin–Worley base-shape
volume plus a higher-frequency Worley detail volume, shaped by a multi-channel
weather map (coverage / type / height) that extends the existing committed
coverage mask and preserves its hero-region floors for KSC and Boca Chica.
Per-sample lighting uses a dual-lobe Henyey-Greenstein phase, a short (≈6-step)
sun-ward transmittance march, LUT transmittance to the top of the atmosphere,
ambient from the multiple-scattering LUT, and a Wrenninge-style
energy-conserving multi-scatter octave approximation. Marching is bounded by
analytic shell intersection, uses coarse-then-refine stepping, and early-outs
once accumulated transmittance drops below a threshold. Output composites
against scene depth so terrain occludes clouds correctly.

**Performance is a tiered, measured contract, not an assumption.** Quality
tiers (march step budget, render resolution scale, temporal reprojection on or
off) are selected by a short startup benchmark. ARCHI records an accepted
30 fps floor; that floor is the gate at both ground level and orbit.

## Implementation Details

### 1. Post-processing: add the tonemap the stack never had

**File**: `apps/web/src/scene/Effects.tsx`

**Current state**: an `EffectComposer` at `resolutionScale={0.5}` containing a
single `Bloom` (threshold 1.0, `mipmapBlur`). Its doc comment states the HDR
contract that the exposure multipliers currently violate.

**Modifications**:
- Add a `ToneMapping` effect after Bloom (ACES or AgX from
  `@react-three/postprocessing`). Bloom must remain *before* the tonemap so it
  operates on scene-referred radiance, which is what its 1.0 threshold assumes.
- Accept the frame-global exposure as a uniform/prop and apply it immediately
  before the tonemap curve.
- Rewrite the doc comment to state the new contract precisely: shaders emit
  radiance normalized to TOA solar irradiance 1.0; exposure and tonemap are
  applied exactly once, here.

### 2. Canvas and the PiP ordering hazard

**File**: `apps/web/src/scene/SceneRoot.tsx`, `apps/web/src/scene/DockingCameraPiP.tsx`

**Current state**: `Canvas` sets no `toneMapping` (three.js default applies).
`Effects` is mounted at `SceneRoot.tsx:173`. `DockingCameraPass` deliberately
runs at render priority 2 *after* the composer (priority 1) because "a negative
priority would draw first and be overwritten by the composer's full-viewport
output."

**Modifications**:
- Set `gl.toneMapping = NoToneMapping` explicitly on the `Canvas` so three does
  not double-apply a curve the composer now owns.
- **Hazard to resolve, not defer:** because the PiP pass draws after the
  composer, it currently bypasses post-processing entirely. Once tonemapping
  moves into the composer the PiP will be visibly mismatched (untonemapped
  radiance). **Decided: PiP stays post-composer (stage 5 of §2b) and gets its
  own exposure + tonemap step sharing the same curve and the same frame
  exposure scalar.** Rendering it into the composer input is rejected — the
  existing priority-2 ordering exists precisely because a pre-composer PiP is
  overwritten by the composer's full-viewport output.

### 2b. Render graph, depth ownership, and composite order

**Files**: `apps/web/src/scene/SceneRoot.tsx`, `apps/web/src/scene/Effects.tsx`,
`apps/web/src/scene/clouds/CloudVolumePass.tsx`, `apps/web/src/scene/Earth.tsx`

**Problem**: the cloud march cannot be a naive post-pass over the finished
frame. By that point the atmosphere shell has already been composited, so there
is no way to place clouds *inside* the atmosphere — in-scatter along the path in
front of a cloud must occlude it, in-scatter behind it must not. The scene also
runs `logarithmicDepthBuffer: true` (`SceneRoot.tsx`), so the depth attachment
is log-encoded and must be linearized before it can bound a march.

**Specified graph**:
0. **Cloud shadow update** (§8b) — sun-space optical-depth map, refreshed only
   when dirty (cloud drift or sun motion, never camera motion). Must run before
   the opaque pass, which samples it. On the first frame, before any update has
   run, the pass binds a fully-lit fallback texture (transmittance 1.0
   everywhere) so terrain is never shadowed by uninitialized data.
1. **Opaque pass** — terrain patches, far globe, craft, starfield → HDR colour
   target plus depth. No atmosphere, no clouds. Surface shaders emit
   **unattenuated** surface radiance (see the doubled-extinction note below).
2. **Sky & clouds pass** — a single fullscreen pass that owns the ray walk.
   Reconstructs linear view depth from the log-depth buffer, then marches the
   atmosphere and both cloud bands along **one ray per pixel**, accumulating in
   physical near-to-far order so cloud and atmosphere in-scatter interleave
   correctly. The march is bounded by reconstructed depth, so terrain occludes
   clouds. Merging the atmosphere march into this pass — rather than leaving it
   on the `atmoFragment` shell mesh — is what makes correct ordering possible,
   and is a required consequence of going volumetric rather than an optional
   refactor.
3. **Bloom** — operates on scene-referred radiance, preserving the existing
   1.0-threshold contract.
4. **Exposure + tonemap** — applied exactly once.
5. **PiP** — must apply the same exposure and tonemap curve (see §2).

**Composite recurrence** per marched segment:
`L_march += T_accum × inScatter_segment`, then
`T_accum ×= exp(-extinction_segment)`, with early-out once `T_accum < 0.01`.

**Terminal composite** against the opaque target:
`L_out = L_march + T_accum × L_opaque` — the marched in-scatter plus the surface
radiance attenuated by the accumulated transmittance along the same ray.

**Log-depth decoding contract**: the opaque pass writes three.js
logarithmic depth (`logarithmicDepthBuffer: true`, and the terrain/cloud
shaders already hand-write the same encoding via `logDepthBufFC`). The sky pass
must invert exactly that encoding — recovering `w` from the stored value using
the same `logDepthBufFC` uniform, then converting to view-space distance — and
must not assume a standard `1/z` mapping. Encoding and decoding share one GLSL
helper so they cannot drift apart.

**Doubled extinction hazard (phase interaction, must not be missed)**: in
Phase 2 the opaque shaders composite aerial perspective themselves
(`lit × skyTransmittanceRatio + skyAerialInScatter`). Once Phase 3 marches the
atmosphere along that *same* camera-to-surface path and composites over the
opaque target, that path would be integrated twice — extinction squared,
in-scatter added twice. Phase 3 therefore **must** strip the aerial composite
out of the opaque shaders and have them emit unattenuated surface radiance,
handing ownership of the camera-to-surface path entirely to the sky pass. This
is a required Phase 3 task, not cleanup.

**Consequence for §6**: the atmosphere raymarch relocates out of the shell mesh
into this pass. The measurement gate still applies, and "half-res" now means
half-resolution for the combined sky-and-cloud pass rather than the shell alone.

### 3. Collapse the exposure configuration

**File**: `apps/web/src/scene/sky/skyConfig.ts`

**Current state**: `SKY_CONFIG.exposure` carries `groundIntensity`,
`spaceIntensity`, `groundAltitudeKm`, `spaceAltitudeKm`, `curvePower`, and
`insideIntensity`. `atmosphereExposureFromAltitudeKm()` derives the per-altitude
multiplier; `ATMOSPHERE_INTENSITY`, `ATMOSPHERE_GROUND_EXPOSURE`, and
`AERIAL_SKY_RADIANCE` are exported as display constants.

**Modifications**:
- Replace the whole `exposure` block with a single camera exposure description.
  **The function is pinned here, because it is the one knob the entire image now
  hangs on:**
  `EV = EV_base + k_sun × log2(max(sunFactor, sunFloor)) + k_alt × altitudeTerm`,
  evaluated once per frame on the CPU, where
  - `sunFactor = saturate(sin(sunElevation) + twilightBias)`, with
    **sun elevation measured at the camera's sub-nadir ground point, not at the
    camera** — measuring at the camera is meaningless in orbit, where the sun
    can be up while the ground below is in night;
  - `altitudeTerm` is a smoothstep over camera altitude from ground to the top
    of the atmosphere;
  - the result is clamped to `[EV_min, EV_max]` and converted to a linear
    multiplier as `exposure = 2^(-EV)`.
  `EV_base`, `k_sun`, `k_alt`, `sunFloor`, `twilightBias`, and the clamp bounds
  live in `SKY_CONFIG`. **Starting defaults** (so the implementer is not
  inventing them, and so the tests have something to bracket):
  `EV_base = 0.0`, `k_sun = 3.0`, `k_alt = 1.5`, `sunFloor = 0.02`,
  `twilightBias = 0.10`, clamp `[EV_min, EV_max] = [-4.0, +6.0]`.
  These are calibration *starting points*, not final values.
- **Calibration produces the test oracle.** Phase 1 tunes against four fixed
  reference framings (noon ground, sunset limb, night, orbit); the resulting EV
  at each is then **committed as a golden constant** in `SKY_CONFIG`. The
  exposure tests assert against those recorded goldens plus the
  framing-independent properties (continuity, monotonic decrease in EV as sun
  elevation falls, clamping at both ends). This keeps the implementer from
  inventing oracle values while being honest that the exact numbers can only
  come from looking at the image.
- Delete `atmosphereExposureFromAltitudeKm()` and the derived
  `ATMOSPHERE_INTENSITY` / `ATMOSPHERE_GROUND_EXPOSURE` /
  `AERIAL_SKY_RADIANCE` display exports once their consumers are converted.
- Add cloud-volume configuration: band altitudes and thicknesses for both
  marched layers, noise frequencies, HG asymmetry parameters, multi-scatter
  octave count, per-tier step budgets and resolution scales. All renderer
  values continue to derive from `SKY_CONFIG` per the ARCHI single-source rule.

### 4. LUT-sampled lighting helpers

**File**: `apps/web/src/scene/sky/lighting.ts`

**Current state**: `SKY_LIGHTING_GLSL` exports `skyTerminatorRamp`,
`skyLightingAmount`, `skySunTint`, `skyExposureCurve`, `densityWarpedDistance`,
and `skyTransmittanceRatio` (whose ray direction was corrected this cycle and
is covered by `lighting.test.ts`).

**Data products available, stated honestly**: both committed LUTs are 2D,
parameterized by (altitude, sun-zenith cosine) — transmittance, and Hillaire's
`Psi_ms` multiple-scattering term. **Neither is a normal-dependent hemispherical
irradiance, and neither is a view-path aerial-perspective volume.** The helpers
below are therefore explicitly named approximations built on those two products,
not exact integrals. Deleting `AERIAL_SKY_RADIANCE` without saying what replaces
it would leave terrain compositing undefined, so its *role* is replaced, not
just removed.

**Units contract**: every radiance and irradiance value is a unitless multiple
of top-of-atmosphere solar irradiance (TOA = 1.0), matching the Phase 1
radiance space.

**Modifications**:
- Add `skySunIrradiance(point, sunDir)` → sun irradiance at `point`, **already
  attenuated** by the transmittance LUT along the sun ray. Consumers must not
  apply transmittance a second time (see §5).
- Add `skyAmbientIrradiance(point, normal)` → isotropic-sky approximation:
  `Psi_ms(altitude, mu_sun) × hemisphereFactor(normal)`, with `hemisphereFactor`
  the cosine-weighted visible-sky fraction, approximated as
  `(1 + dot(normal, up)) / 2`.
  **There is deliberately no `× sunIrradiance` factor here.** The committed
  `Psi_ms` bake is already seeded with the sun transmittance at the sample —
  `Earth.tsx` documents this explicitly ("must NOT be re-multiplied by
  sunTransmittance here (that squares the twilight falloff)") — and under the
  TOA = 1.0 units contract the solar term is unity anyway. Multiplying by the
  already-attenuated `skySunIrradiance` would reintroduce exactly the squared
  twilight falloff this plan removes elsewhere.
  Documented in-code as an approximation; a baked cosine-weighted irradiance
  LUT is the principled upgrade, recorded as a follow-up.
- Add `skyAerialInScatter(cameraPoint, surfacePoint)` → view-path in-scatter
  replacing the hardcoded `AERIAL_SKY_RADIANCE` value: sky radiance from
  `Psi_ms` at the path-midpoint altitude, scaled by
  `(1 - skyTransmittanceRatio)`. This retains the existing, already-tested
  transmittance-ratio machinery and substitutes a LUT-derived colour for the
  hardcoded constant. The Hillaire 3D froxel aerial-perspective volume remains
  the principled replacement and is scoped as a follow-up, not silently assumed.
- Delete `skyLightingAmount`, `skyTerminatorRamp`, `skySunTint`, and
  `skyExposureCurve` once consumers are converted.
- Retain `skyTransmittanceRatio` and `densityWarpedDistance`.

### 5. Terrain shading

**File**: `apps/web/src/scene/terrain/terrainShaders.ts`

**Current state**: `lit = surface * skyLightingAmount(ndotl) * skySunTint(ndotl)`,
then `color = lit * transmittance + AERIAL_SKY_RADIANCE * (1 - transmittance)`.
The `EXPOSURE_*` GLSL constants are already removed from this file; the aerial
term is currently unscaled.

**Modifications**:
- Replace `lit` with
  `albedo/π × (skySunIrradiance(point, sunDir) × NdotL + skyAmbientIrradiance(point, n))`.
  **There is deliberately no second transmittance factor**: `skySunIrradiance`
  already carries it, and multiplying again would square the twilight
  attenuation and darken the terminator incorrectly.
- Composite aerial perspective as
  `lit × skyTransmittanceRatio + skyAerialInScatter(...)`.
- **Cloud shadows, phased deliberately.** The end state is the sun-space cloud
  shadow map (§8b): a coverage lookup is not an optical depth, so it cannot
  agree with what the march produces; only an integrated sun-column
  transmittance can. But that map depends on the volumetric density assets,
  which land in Phase 3. **Phase 2 therefore keeps the existing coverage-based
  shadow untouched** — it is no worse than today and keeps Phase 2
  independently shippable — and the cutover to the shadow map happens in Phase 3
  alongside the density field it needs. Terrain and the far globe then consume
  the same shadow texture.
- `createWaterMaterial` / `WATER_FRAGMENT_SHADER` in this same file is a second
  consumer of the shared lighting helpers and must be converted alongside the
  terrain material, or ocean and land will disagree.

### 6. Atmosphere shell

**File**: `apps/web/src/scene/Earth.tsx`

**Current state**: `atmoFragment` raymarches the shell (`ATMOSPHERE_INSIDE_STEPS`
= 32 inside, 12 outside), multiplies by the altitude exposure curve — currently
blended across the top tenth of the shell as a stopgap for the flash — then
applies its own `1 - exp(-scattered)` rolloff. `earthFragment` renders the far
globe with `farGlobeOpacity` and its own aerial in-scatter.

**Modifications**:
- Delete the in-shader exposure entirely (curve, `EXPOSURE_*` constants, and
  the `shellFraction` stopgap blend); emit radiance and let the composer apply
  exposure and tonemap.
- Remove the local `1 - exp(-scattered)` rolloff; the tonemap now owns that.
- Convert `earthFragment`'s lighting to the LUT helpers for consistency with
  terrain.
- **Measurement gate (owner decision):** instrument this pass and measure it on
  the weak-hardware target before optimizing. Only if it fails budget, add
  half-resolution rendering plus a depth-aware upsample. Do not pre-emptively
  optimize — the fps figures gathered during the previous debugging session
  were confounded by browser tab throttling and are not trustworthy evidence.

### 7. Baked cloud assets

**Files**: `apps/web/scripts/makeCloudNoise.mjs` (new),
`apps/web/scripts/makeWeatherMap.mjs` (new),
`apps/web/public/assets/ASSETS.md`

**Current state**: `scripts/bakeAtmosphere.mjs` is the established pattern —
deterministic bake, committed binary under `public/assets/`, provenance row in
`ASSETS.md`, determinism test in the suite. A single-channel cloud coverage
mask already exists with hero-region caps for KSC and Boca Chica.

**Modifications**:
- `makeCloudNoise.mjs`: bake a tiling Perlin–Worley base-shape volume (128³,
  RGBA: low-frequency Perlin-Worley plus three Worley octaves) and a Worley
  detail volume (32³). Both seeded and deterministic.
- **Format decided, not merely flagged**: both volumes ship as filterable
  **RGBA8** `Data3DTexture` payloads — WebGL2 cannot linearly filter RGB float,
  the same constraint the committed atmosphere LUTs already document, and the
  detail volume is padded to RGBA even though only three channels carry data.
  All three axes use `RepeatWrapping` so the noise tiles; `LinearFilter` on
  both min and mag; no sRGB decode (these are data, not colour).
- **Committed artifact paths**: `apps/web/public/assets/clouds/cloud_shape.bin`
  (128³ RGBA8), `apps/web/public/assets/clouds/cloud_detail.bin` (32³ RGBA8),
  `apps/web/public/assets/clouds/weather.png` (multi-channel weather map).
- `makeWeatherMap.mjs`: extend the existing coverage bake to multi-channel
  (R = coverage, G = cloud type stratus↔cumulus, B = cloud-top height), keeping
  the hero-region coverage floors and using the shared equirect UV convention
  mandated by ARCHI (`cloudSphericalUv`, −x̂ → u=0). This convention is exactly
  what the antipodal hero-cap bug violated; the registration test is the guard.
- Add provenance rows for both assets to `ASSETS.md`.

### 8. The cloud volume module

**Files**: `apps/web/src/scene/clouds/cloudVolume.ts` (new),
`apps/web/src/scene/clouds/CloudVolumePass.tsx` (new),
`apps/web/src/scene/clouds/cloudShaders.ts` (new)

**Design**:
- `cloudVolume.ts` holds the **pure, testable math**, mirroring how
  `atmosphereMath.ts` and `cloudPlacement.ts` keep analytic cores out of GLSL so
  they can carry oracle tests: HG phase (single and dual-lobe), Beer–Lambert
  transmittance, the multi-scatter octave weights, density remapping from
  weather-map channels, and shell-intersection ray bounds.
- `cloudShaders.ts` holds the GLSL march, importing shared helpers from
  `lighting.ts` and `cloudCoverage.ts` so CPU and GPU agree (the existing
  CPU/GPU transfer-function pinning test is the precedent).
- `CloudVolumePass.tsx` owns the render target, tier selection, optional
  temporal reprojection history buffer, and depth-aware composite.
- March structure: analytic entry/exit against each band's shell; coarse
  stepping until a density hit, then refine; early-out at transmittance < 0.01;
  per-sample sun-ward march (~6 steps) for self-shadowing.
- Both bands are marched (owner selected fully volumetric). Cirrus uses a
  thinner band, lower step budget, and a type-biased density remap rather than
  a separate code path.

### 8b. Cloud shadow map

**File**: `apps/web/src/scene/clouds/CloudShadowPass.tsx` (new)

A low-resolution sun-space transmittance texture covering the cloud bands,
produced by a short optical-depth march along the sun direction, and consumed by
terrain (§5), the far globe, and the volumetric pass's ambient term.

This exists because sampling the weather map at the shading point is **not** a
shadow: coverage is not an optical depth, and only an integrated sun-column
transmittance can agree with what the march produces. Updated at a reduced rate
— it changes only with cloud drift and sun motion, not camera motion — and its
cost counts against the tier budget.

### 9. Quality tiers and the benchmark

**File**: `apps/web/src/scene/clouds/cloudQuality.ts` (new)

**Design**: a short startup benchmark selects a tier (step budget, resolution
scale, reprojection on/off). Manual override exposed for debugging and for
capturing release screenshots at a fixed tier.

**Temporal reprojection hazard**: DEBUG FLY reaches 200 km/s
(`flySpeedPerAgl.maxMps`). Reprojection must be gated on camera velocity and
disabled above a threshold, falling back to a higher sample count or accepting
noise, or it will ghost catastrophically during descent.

**History invalidation (required, and broader than velocity)**: velocity gating
alone is insufficient. The reprojection history must be discarded outright on:

- **floating-origin rebase** — `WorldFrame` re-anchors when the camera drifts
  past the rebase threshold, shifting every render-space coordinate at once and
  invalidating all reprojection vectors;
- camera cuts and view-mode changes (CINEMATIC/CHASE/COCKPIT/DEBUG, PiP toggles);
- render-target resize;
- quality-tier changes.

**Angular** velocity must be gated alongside translational velocity — a fast
rotation invalidates reprojection just as thoroughly as fast translation, and
the DEBUG FLY free-look can produce one without the other.

## Technical Considerations

- **Pattern Usage**: follows the established sky pipeline shape from ARCHI —
  `skyConfig.ts` single source → in-code derivations → baked committed assets
  via `scripts/*.mjs` with `ASSETS.md` provenance → analytic core in pure TS
  with oracle tests → GLSL consuming shared helpers.
- **Package Boundary**: entirely within `apps/web`. `sim-core` and `scenario`
  are untouched; no sim-core internals are imported. FSW/TruthState are not
  involved.
- **Conventions**: the shared equirect UV convention (−x̂ → u=0, +x̂ → u=0.5)
  governs every weather-map and density lookup; renderer values derive from
  `SKY_CONFIG`, never free-standing constants.
- **Determinism**: noise and weather-map bakes are seeded and reproducible, and
  are covered by bake-determinism tests. Cloud animation is render-time drift
  only and must not couple to sim time — the existing `Clouds.tsx` comment
  ("Render-side only; never sim time") states the rule that carries over.
  Temporal reprojection is frame-history state and must not feed anything
  observable by the sim or telemetry.
- **Performance**: 30 fps floor accepted by the owner (ARCHI roadmap item 7),
  measured at ground level and in orbit on the weak-hardware target. Heavy work
  stays on the GPU; no per-frame main-thread cost is added to the render loop.
- **Edge Cases**: camera inside a cloud band; camera below cloud base at ground
  level; terrain occluding clouds (depth composite); sun at and below the
  horizon; night; the far-globe/terrain crossfade band; band intersection when
  the two marched layers overlap in screen space.

## Type Definitions

- `CloudQualityTier` — step budgets (primary + sun march), resolution scale,
  reprojection enabled, velocity cutoff for reprojection.
- `CloudBandConfig` — inner/outer altitude, density scale, noise frequencies,
  HG asymmetry pair and blend, type bias (cumulus vs cirrus).
- `WeatherSample` — coverage, type, height, decoded from the map's channels.
- `CloudMarchResult` — accumulated scattering and transmittance, for the pure
  core and its tests.

## Performance & Cost Impact

Volumetric marching replaces transparent overdraw with bounded per-pixel march
cost, which is the more controllable of the two on weak hardware — but marching
*both* bands raises the ceiling, so the tier system carries real weight here.
Expected: a startup benchmark cost of a few frames; steady-state cost dominated
by the primary march (step budget × resolution scale) plus the sun-ward march.
The atmosphere shell may also need half-resolution treatment, decided by
measurement (§6). Deleting 12,000 instanced billboards and two full-sphere
transparent shells removes a substantial overdraw load, which partially offsets
the march.

## Backward Compatibility

No API or data-format compatibility concerns — this is renderer-internal.
The visual change is large and deliberate: v0.8.0 checkpoint framings and any
release screenshots become stale references and must be re-captured. Exposure
and lighting change globally, so every existing visual reference shifts.

## Test Impact

- **New oracle tests** (pure core in `cloudVolume.ts`): HG phase normalizes to 1
  over the sphere; Beer–Lambert transmittance is monotonic and bounded in [0,1];
  shell-intersection bounds are correct for camera outside, inside, and below
  each band.
- **Multi-scatter energy invariant, stated correctly**: the test is that total
  outgoing scattered radiance plus transmitted radiance does not exceed incident
  radiance. "Never exceeds single-scatter" is **not** the invariant — higher
  scattering orders may legitimately exceed first-order magnitude while still
  conserving energy. The pure core must declare whether it returns *additional*
  or *total* radiance, and the test asserts against that declaration.
- **New exposure tests**: the frame exposure function is continuous and
  monotonic in sun elevation, clamps at both endpoints, and produces the
  expected EV at the four reference conditions (noon ground, sunset limb, night,
  orbit).
- **New bake tests**: noise and weather-map bakes are deterministic and tile
  seamlessly; weather-map UV registration matches the shared convention and the
  hero-region coverage floors hold — porting the intent of the existing
  `cloudPlacement.test.ts` registration test, which must not be dropped when
  billboard placement is retired.
- **New continuity regression**: frame exposure is continuous across the
  atmosphere shell boundary and across the terrain crossfade band. This directly
  pins the flash bug that a discontinuous inside/outside exposure branch caused.
- **Existing tests affected**: `skyConfig.test.ts` (exposure fields removed —
  expectations must be rewritten, not merely deleted); `cloudPlacement.test.ts`
  (billboard placement retired); `lighting.test.ts` (added this cycle; the
  `skyTransmittanceRatio` direction assertions remain valid);
  `atmosphereMath.test.ts` unaffected.
- **Integration check**: a manual descent capture sequence (orbit → cloud layer →
  ground) at a fixed quality tier, plus recorded fps at ground and orbit against
  the 30 fps floor. GLSL correctness is not unit-testable and is judged by
  rendered output, consistent with the existing coverage-debt policy.
